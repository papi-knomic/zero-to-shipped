The problem

Expired paperwork is expensive.

A typical small business holds a dozen documents that quietly expire: tax clearance and company filings, fire safety certificates, insurance policies, premises permits and leases. The dates are buried in PDFs and phone scans, nobody owns the calendar, and one missed renewal can mean a fine, a sealed shop or an uninsured loss.

01

Dates hide in documents

Some documents print an expiry date. Others only say “valid for twelve months from the date of issue”, and someone has to do the maths.

02

Nobody owns the calendar

Renewals come round once a year, often long after whoever filed the paperwork has moved on to other work.

03

Lapses cost real money

Penalties, lost contracts that require valid certificates, and claims refused because the cover had already lapsed.

How it works

Four steps, then you can forget about it.

  1. 1

    Upload

    Drop in a PDF or a photo. It goes straight to private storage through a short-lived upload link.

  2. 2

    Lapse reads it

    Lapse finds the document type, issuer, parties and every date, along with the exact line each date came from. “Valid for 12 months” becomes an exact expiry date, calculated rather than guessed.

  3. 3

    You confirm

    Nothing is scheduled until you’ve checked the details against the document and fixed anything that’s off.

  4. 4

    Reminders go out

    Email reminders 60, 30 and 7 days before expiry, plus an in-app feed so nothing is missed if an email is.

Security

Your documents are safe with us.

Licences, policies and contracts are sensitive. Lapse is built to keep them private, and to be clear about where they are.

Private to your account
Only you can see your documents. Every request is checked against your signed-in account.
Encrypted, always
Documents are stored encrypted, and every connection to Lapse is encrypted too.
Nothing happens without you
No reminder is scheduled until you’ve checked the details. Reminders go only to the address you choose.
Where your data lives
Lapse runs on Amazon Web Services. Your documents are stored in AWS’s US East region.
